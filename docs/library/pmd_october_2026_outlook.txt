Current Meteorological Conditions Below-normal rainfall was observed across most parts of the country during September . Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Punjab, however, received rainfall close to the climatological normal record. Gilgit-Baltistan, Balochistan, and Sindh remained predominantly dry , with Sindh experiencing particularly deficient rainfall during the month and receiving only trace amounts at isolated locations. Three rainfall spells affected the country during September, with their impacts mainly concentrated over the northern and upper parts, while the southern half of the country experienced comparatively little rainfall. Meanwhile, temperatures remained above normal across most parts of the country, with the largest positive temperature anomalies recorded over Balochistan, followed by Sindh during September (Table ). Monthly Rainfall Outlook: The monthly outlook is derived from the ensemble mean of selected global seasonal prediction models, providing the principal guidance for operational rainfall and temperature forecasts. Current model projections indicate the persistence of positive El Niño Souther n Oscillation (ENSO) conditions through October , alongside continued positive IOD conditions over the Indian Ocean , although ENSO and IOD may exert partly compensating influences on precipitation over Pakistan. Nevertheless, based on these analyses, the model outlook indicates near-normal to above -normal* rainfall over the northern parts of the country, particularly northern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, adjoining parts of Gilgit-Baltistan, Azad Jammu and Kashmir and northern Punjab during October . Near normal to slightly below normal rainfall is anticipated across central to southern parts of the country with the most pronounced negative anomalies expected over southern parts of Sindh. Overall, the outlook indicates a clear north–south gradient in rainfall anomalies with chances of isolated rainfall events mostly over the northern half of the country during October (Figures and ). Impacts:  Dry weather conditions in southern regions are favorable for the post -harvest activities of Kharif crops, particularly rice, cotton, maize and groundnut.  Above-normal temperatures in upper glaciated regions may contribute to higher than normal flows at vulnerable locations, although the overall probability remains low.  Spatial temperature gradient may cause strong winds, dust storms, thunderstorm and hailstorms which may affect seasonal crops, vegetables, and orchards; farmers are advised to take precautionary measures to protect standing crops.  Isolated showers, hailstorms and strong winds may occasionally disrupt Kharif post -harvesting and early Rabi sowing activities.  Warm and humid environment would support the breeding of Dengue larva in central Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northwestern parts of Punjab. Dengue outbreak due to stagnant water is likely in inundated areas.  Dry conditions, coupled with above -normal temperatures, may contribute to deteriorating air quality and smog formation over the central and southern plains and urban centers of the country, potentially posing health risks to sensitive groups, particularly children and the elderly.  Advisory for general public:  Farmers are advised to visit PMD advisories on website before irrigation or sowing  Travelers and tourists are advised to remain cautious while travelling during rainfall events.  Subscribe PMD Channels on WhatsApp Application to remain updated with changing weather conditions. Note: The seasonal outlook is updated monthly in the first week of the month. The forecast reliability varies with location, time of year, and global ocean/atmospheric conditions. It provides general trends using probabilities rather than precise predictions and compares expected conditions to historical averages. For better decision -making, it should be used alongside short-term forecasts and other climate data issued by PMD.
