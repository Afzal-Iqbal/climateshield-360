Current Meteorological Conditions During the July–September (JAS) season, rainfall remained predominantly below normal across the country. The rainfall observed in Punjab and Gilgit-Baltistan, however, remained close to the climatological record . The southern half of the country experienced predominantly below -normal rainfall, with Sindh recording significantly defici ent rainfall during the season. Following the onset of the summer monsoon on st July, multiple rainfall spells of moderate to heavy intensity affected various parts of the co untry, particularly the northern regions. Isolated episodes of heavy rainfall triggered flash and urban flooding in low-lying areas of Punjab, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. A notable rainfall spell during the first week of August affected most parts of Sindh and the southern parts of Balochistan. However, no significant rainfall spells occurred thereafter, resulting in prolonged dry conditions over these regions. Temperatures during JAS remained predominantly above normal across most parts of Pakistan, while Gilgit-Baltistan experienced near -normal temperatures during the season. The largest positive seasonal temperature anomalies were observed over Balochistan, followed by Sindh (Table ). The model outputs are integrated using the Multi -Model Ensemble (MME) technique, resulting in an operational outlook for seasonal rainfall and temperature. Currently, the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) is in a positive phase and is expected to shift to its neutral phase at the end of the OND season. Likewise, the current positive phase of the El Niño –Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is expected to persist throughout the season. Based on the prevailing climatic conditions, the forecast indicates a general tendency for normal to above-normal* rainfall in most parts of the country during October to December (OND) . The forecast indicates a pronounced positive anomaly concentrated over northwestern regions, with the highest anomalies expected across central to northern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and adjoining areas of Gilgit-Baltistan, Kashmir, and northern Punjab. Near normal to slightly below normal rainfall is anticipated across central to southern parts of the country with weak negative anomalies expected over southern parts of Sindh (Figures and ). Impacts:  Agricultural Advisory: o Isolated showers, hailstorms, or windstorms mainly during October may disrupt Kharif harvesting and post-harvest activities, particularly in northwestern regions. Farmers are advised to take precautionary measures to minimize potential damage. o Favorable rainfall conditions in northwestern regions may support timely Rabi sowing, while dryer conditions in southern regions may increase irrigation requirements during the early Rabi season.  Air Quality: o Near to below normal rainfall in central to southern Pakistan, may lead to an increased risk of fog and smog formation in plain areas, potentially resulting in deteriorated air quality and associated impacts on human health and visibility.  Public Health and Wellbeing: o Dengue transmission may remain active during October and November, particularly in warmer and humid urban areas, but is expected to decline towards December. Preventive measures, including vector control and removal of stagnant water, should be strengthened to reduce dengue transmission.  Snowmelt, Avalanches o Above-normal temperatures in Gilgit -Baltistan, northern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Kashmir may delay snow accumulation at lower elevations, while snowfall at higher elevations may gradually establish seasonal snow cover. o Periods of sn owfall and rainfall in high -altitude areas, particularly from November onward, may contribute to unstable snowpack conditions and localized avalanches, especially in steep and vulnerable terrain. Note: The Seasonal Outlook is updated monthly in the first week of the month. The forecast reliability varies with location, time of year, and global ocean/atmospheric conditions. It provides general trends using probabilities rather than precise predictions and compares expected conditions to historical averages. For better decision-making, it should be used alongside short-term forecasts and other climate data issued by PMD.
